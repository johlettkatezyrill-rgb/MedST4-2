    💗 Heart failure basics
    📝 From your instructor
    • Know the definition of heart failure
    • HTN and CAD can strain the heart and lead to HF
    • Risk factors: obesity, smoking, diabetes
    Definition: the heart can't pump enough blood to meet the body's needs. It does not mean the heart has stopped.
    🫀 Left vs right-sided HF
    📝 From your instructor

    Left-sided

    • Dyspnea
    • Orthopnea
    • Pink frothy sputum
    • PE (pulmonary edema)
    • Crackles

    Right-sided

    Backfills to the body

    • Dependent edema
    • JVD
    • Increased abdominal girth
    • Biventricular HF = both sides
    Memory trick: Left = Lungs. Right = the rest of the body (Rest of body).
    🩺 Assessing & what to do
    📝 From your instructor
    • Ask: “Do you have to sit up when you're sleeping? How many pillows do you use at night?”
    • New or worsening dyspnea: check work of breathing (assess O₂, lung sounds, breath quality: shallow? deep?)
    • Acute respiratory distress: sit the patient upright if SOB, give O₂ as needed
    • Monitor: I&O, weight gain, edema, oxygenation, changes in breath sounds, adventitious breath sounds
    🏡 Teaching (at home)
    📝 From your instructor
    • Weigh after urination
    • Report rapid weight gain, increased swelling, and SOB
    • Stick to prescribed salt/fluid goals (look for sodium per serving)
    • Oxygen maintenance: don't smoke near the tank
    Best habit: same scale, same time each morning (after urinating, before eating), similar clothing.
    💊 Medications
    📝 From your instructor

    Beta blockers

    • Drop BP and HR
    • Can cause bronchospasm in patients with asthma
    • Orthostatic hypotension → fall risk

    “-prils” (ACE inhibitors)

    • Check potassium and kidney function
    • Angioedema: not an allergic reaction, it's an adverse reaction
    Angioedema can close the airway: protect the airway and call the provider right away. ACE inhibitors raise potassium (hyperkalemia).

    Furosemide

    • Removes excess sodium
    • Monitor weight, BP, kidney function, electrolytes
    • K⁺: know the symptoms of hypokalemia (constipation, dysrhythmias, palpitations)
    • Also watch Mg
    It also removes water, which is why it helps with fluid overload. Good sign it's working: more urine, fewer crackles, weight down.

    Digoxin

    • Toxicity: nausea, yellow/green halos, anorexia, new appetite loss, low HR
    • Check apical pulse before giving
    • Check kidney function, electrolytes; if labs are available, look at potassium
    • If HR is 61, you can still give digoxin
    The usual cutoff is to hold if the apical pulse is under 60 (check your facility policy). Low potassium makes digoxin toxicity more likely.
    🔧 Procedures & tests
    📝 From your instructor
    • Cardioversion: helps restore irregular rhythm (usually a fast-paced arrhythmia)
    • Pacemaker: CRT / ICD
    • Percutaneous insertion of a balloon, aortic balloon
    • Echocardiogram
    Cardioversion = a synchronized shock. CRT = biventricular pacing so both ventricles squeeze together. ICD = detects dangerous rhythms and shocks or paces. Echo = ultrasound of the heart's structure and pumping (ejection fraction).
    🩰 Hypertension quick review
    🌷 Extra review
    Adult categorySystolicDiastolic
    Normal< 120and < 80
    Elevated120–129and < 80
    Stage 1130–139or 80–89
    Stage 2≥ 140or ≥ 90
    Crisis> 180and/or > 120
    • Urgency: crisis-level BP without new organ damage. Emergency: crisis-level BP with organ damage (headache, vision changes, confusion, chest pain, SOB). Emergency = IV meds with continuous monitoring, lowering BP gradually.
    • Target organ damage: brain (stroke), heart (LVH, MI, HF), kidneys (↑ creatinine), eyes (retinopathy, papilledema)
    • Nonmodifiable risks: age, family history, ethnicity. Modifiable: obesity, smoking, sodium, alcohol, inactivity
    • Lifestyle: DASH or Mediterranean-style eating, sodium under 2,300 mg/day, aerobic exercise, limit alcohol, quit smoking, weight loss
    • Take BP meds even when feeling fine. If BP stays high, first ask how and when meds are taken
